"Use keyset pagination, OFFSET is slow." I measured it. The honest answer: it depends who is reading the pages.

A person clicking through a list rarely goes past page 10. On a 1,000,000-row PostgreSQL table, pages 1 to 11 cost OFFSET 4 to 10 buffers and keyset 4 or 5, a few hundredths of a millisecond either way. Keyset asks for a unique tiebreaker, an index per sort order, a cursor in the client, and no "go to page 37". For that reader it does not pay for itself.

A machine reading every page, an export, a sync, another service, is a different reader:

- Every page of 1,000,000 rows: OFFSET 56 s, keyset 2.5 s. Page k re-reads the k-1 pages before it, so the cost grows with the square of the table.
- Other requests' data left in the shared cache after 30 s beside the walker: 56% with OFFSET, 100% with keyset.
- Rows an export repeated while new rows arrived: 6,609 with OFFSET, 0 with keyset.

Neither is the good one. They answer different questions.
